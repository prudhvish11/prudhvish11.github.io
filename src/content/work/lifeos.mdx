LifeOS started as a question: what would it look like to run daily life — habits, goals, the day-to-day operating layer — the way you'd design a real product, instead of another to-do list.

## The idea

Most personal-organization apps are a single list wearing different skins. LifeOS is built around the idea that a life has structure — routines, goals, reflection — and the software should reflect that structure rather than flatten it into one undifferentiated list.

## Information architecture and UX

The direction, the information architecture, the UX decisions, and the iteration on all of it are mine. That's the part of "building a product" that doesn't disappear just because AI wrote the implementation.

## Building with AI-assisted development

LifeOS was implemented through an AI-assisted development workflow: prompting, direction-setting, testing, and iterating toward a working product, rather than hand-writing every layer conventionally. The product decisions are still product decisions.
