## 01 / Why I built it

LifeOS brings planning, habits, health, study, career, finance and everyday tracking into one personal system instead of scattering them across unrelated tools. Most personal-organization apps are a single list wearing different skins; LifeOS is built around the idea that a life has structure, and the software should reflect that structure rather than flatten it.

## 02 / Information architecture

The real navigation, turned into a clean diagram — Home, Tasks, Planner, Progress, Goals and Notes at the top level, with a dedicated Modules area for the seven life areas the product tracks.

## 03 / Module system

Body, Nutrition, Workout, Water, Study, Career and Finance each get their own module rather than being crammed into one generic tracker — the point is that a life has more than one kind of thing worth tracking.

## 04 / Product flow

The dashboard reduces to four systems: capture (Quick Track), plan (Tasks, Planner, Upcoming), measure (Progress, weekly summaries), and the life modules themselves. That's what makes the screen legible instead of just busy.

## 05 / AI-assisted build process

**Role** — concept, feature definition, information architecture, UX direction, design decisions, testing, iteration.
**Implementation** — AI-assisted development, not hand-written line-by-line.

The product decisions are still product decisions; AI-assisted doesn't mean AI-directed.

## 06 / What I learned

Structure is the hard part. Once the module boundaries were right, the UI mostly followed — the design work was in deciding what belonged where, not in the visual layer on top.

## 07 / Live product

LifeOS is a live, authenticated product — sign-in protects real user data, so this case study shows the real dashboard rather than an authenticated walkthrough.
